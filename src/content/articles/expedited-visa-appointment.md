**A U.S. embassy or consulate may consider an expedited nonimmigrant visa interview for an urgent, unforeseen situation, but the request process, evidence standard and available appointments are controlled by the location where the interview will occur.** The Department of State says applicants must first submit the DS-160, pay the application fee and schedule the first available regular interview before the consular section reviews an emergency expedite request.

An expedited interview is not expedited visa issuance. It does not guarantee an appointment, approval, completion of administrative processing or passport return by a travel date.

## Three ways an earlier interview may arise

| Path | Who it is for | What it can do | Main limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Emergency expedite request | Applicants with an urgent, unforeseen situation; the Department gives examples such as a funeral, medical emergency or school start date | Allows the post to review a request for an earlier interview after the ordinary application steps are completed | Criteria, proof, request method and availability vary by post; approval is discretionary |
| Earlier regular slot | An applicant who already has an appointment and sees an earlier opening in the scheduling system | Allows rescheduling to a newly released regular appointment, if the system permits | Slots change and are not guaranteed; use only the official post or scheduling system |
| Temporary paid-expedite pilot | Certain B-visa applicants at selected participating locations during the announced pilot | May offer an earlier interview option when the feature, eligibility and inventory are available | It is not global, is time-limited, has a separate non-refundable fee and does not affect visa adjudication |

## General emergency-request sequence

The Department's [nonimmigrant visa processing guidance](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/immigrate/the-immigrant-visa-process/step-1-submit-a-petition/step-2-begin-nvc-processing/helpful-hints-niv-processing.html) establishes this general order:

1. Complete and submit the DS-160.
2. Pay the applicable nonimmigrant visa application fee.
3. Schedule the first available regular interview appointment.
4. Follow the expedite-request instructions on the website of the embassy or consulate where the interview will occur, or in its officially designated scheduling system.
5. Provide proof explaining why an earlier appointment is needed.
6. Wait for the consular section's decision before treating the appointment as changed.

Do not skip directly to a form or email copied from a forum. Start from the official embassy or consulate site so that the request goes through the correct location and provider.

## What may support an emergency request

The Department gives examples of urgent, unforeseen situations such as a funeral, medical emergency or school start date. These examples are not an exhaustive global eligibility rule, and a stated reason does not automatically qualify. Each post may define required evidence and operational limits.

A useful request should be truthful, specific and supported by the evidence requested by that post. VisaOS cannot draft facts, determine whether a reason is urgent enough or submit the request on an applicant's behalf.

## Temporary paid-expedite pilot: high-volatility notice

As verified on October 29, 2026, the Department of State's [expedited appointment pilot page](https://travel.state.gov/en/newsroom/visas/2026/nonimmigrant-visa-expedited-appointment-pilot-program.html) describes an optional program at selected embassies and consulates through **December 31, 2026**.

The page says eligible B-visa applicants with a future regular interview and an unexpired fee receipt may see a **Paid Expedite** option when the location participates and appointments are available. It currently lists a separate **US$750 fee per applicant** and an interview within the next **10 business days**, subject to availability. The fee is described as non-refundable and non-transferable, and the appointment cannot be rescheduled under the pilot rules.

Those amounts, dates, locations and system rules are volatile. The option may not appear outside participating countries or when appointment inventory is unavailable. It applies to an earlier interview only and does not guarantee visa issuance or expedited processing after the interview. This section must be rechecked immediately before any publication decision and removed or rewritten when the pilot changes or ends.

## Practical scenarios

### A medical event requires urgent travel

Schedule the first available regular appointment, then use the responsible post's official expedite process and provide the proof it requests. Do not purchase non-refundable travel based on the request alone.

### The regular appointment is later than preferred, but there is no emergency

Check the official scheduling system for newly released regular slots. The Department's [global wait-time page](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/global-visa-wait-times.html) says posts add appointments and applicants may be able to move earlier, but its estimates do not guarantee availability. See [how VisaOS interprets wait-time figures](/us/visa-wait-times/).

### A website offers a paid “expedite” outside the official account

Do not assume it is the Department's pilot. The official option appears only through participating locations and the designated scheduling flow described on the government page. VisaOS does not sell appointments, collect expedite fees or recommend unofficial slot brokers.

### The expedite request is denied

The existing regular appointment remains the relevant path unless the official system or post says otherwise. A denied emergency request does not establish the outcome of the later visa interview. Where the temporary paid pilot is available, its official page currently describes separate eligibility, but availability and payment still do not guarantee visa issuance.
