**A refusal under section 214(b) of the Immigration and Nationality Act means the consular officer concluded that the applicant did not establish eligibility for the requested nonimmigrant category and/or did not overcome the applicable presumption of immigrant intent.** It is a decision on that specific application. The Department of State says there is no appeal process and the closed case cannot be reopened by the consular section.

The Department's [Visa Denials guidance](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/visa-denials.html) is the primary plain-language source. The Department also publishes the relevant statutory text in [Ineligibilities and Waivers: Laws](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/waivers.html).

## Two findings can lead to 214(b)

| Possible finding | What it means in the official guidance | What it does not mean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The applicant did not sufficiently demonstrate qualification for the requested nonimmigrant visa category | The stated purpose and facts did not establish eligibility for that category to the officer's satisfaction. | It does not identify one document that will automatically fix a later application. |
| The applicant did not overcome the applicable presumption of immigrant intent | The applicant did not sufficiently demonstrate circumstances and ties supporting departure after the temporary stay. | It does not mean that property, employment, family relationships or a bank balance guarantees approval. |

Section 214(b) applies to nonimmigrant visas, but the exact immigrant-intent rule has statutory category exceptions. The Department's page specifically notes exceptions for H-1B and L applicants and certain accompanying family members. A reader should not apply visitor-visa “strong ties” wording mechanically to every nonimmigrant category.

## “Strong ties” are not a universal checklist

The Department gives examples such as a job, home and relationships with family and friends. It also says officers consider each application individually, including the applicant's circumstances, travel plans, financial resources and ties outside the United States.

Those examples are factors, not required documents or a scoring formula. VisaOS cannot assess whether a person's ties are sufficient, and no third party can guarantee that adding a particular letter, bank statement or property record will change the result.

## 214(b) compared with 221(g)

| Question | 214(b) | 221(g) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current result | Refusal because eligibility for the category and/or the applicable temporary-intent requirement was not established | Refusal because required information is incomplete or the case requires additional administrative processing |
| Can the same case continue? | The Department says the case is closed and the consular section cannot take further action | The case may be reconsidered after requested information is supplied or administrative processing is completed |
| Is there an appeal? | No appeal process | Not an appeal; the applicant follows the officer's document or processing instructions |
| Typical next action | Reapply only through a new application, fee and interview if the applicant chooses | Respond as instructed or wait for the post's administrative-processing communication |

## Reapplying is a new application

The Department says a person may reapply after a 214(b) refusal, but must complete a new application form, pay the application fee and schedule a new interview. It says an applicant should be able to present additional relevant information or significant changes in circumstances since the previous application.

This is not a promise that waiting a certain number of days, changing the wording of an answer or presenting more documents will result in issuance. The official guidance does not prescribe a mandatory waiting period, and the application fee is non-refundable.

## Practical scenarios

### The refusal sheet cites 214(b) after a visitor visa interview

Read the cited legal section and the official denial page. Compare the genuine trip purpose and circumstances with the visitor classification explained in the VisaOS [B-1, B-2 and B-1/B-2 guide](/us/visitor-visa/). Do not invent a different purpose or hide a material fact for a new application.

### A relative wants to contact the embassy for an explanation

The Department says visa records are confidential under INA 222(f), subject to limited exceptions. A friend or relative should not expect the consular section to disclose a case simply because they ask. VisaOS cannot access the refusal record.

### Nothing material has changed

The official page permits reapplication but points to additional information or significant changed circumstances. It does not say that immediately repeating the same application improves the outcome. Whether and when to reapply is an individual decision, not something VisaOS can predict.

## When legal advice may be appropriate

If the refusal involves possible misrepresentation, prior immigration history, criminal issues or uncertainty about the correct visa classification, the question may extend beyond a basic 214(b) explanation. Use the official refusal notice and consider advice from a qualified U.S. immigration lawyer; do not post case records or personal identifiers in a public forum.
