**A 221(g) decision is a visa refusal because the consular officer could not conclude that the applicant was eligible on the available record.** The Department of State describes two common paths: the applicant must provide specified missing information, or the case requires additional administrative processing. A 221(g) case may later be reconsidered, but neither issuance nor a completion date is guaranteed.

Start with the Department's [Visa Denials explanation](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/visa-denials.html) and its separate [Administrative Processing Information](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/administrative-processing-information.html).

## Read the officer's instruction first

| What the applicant was told | What the official guidance says | Appropriate next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Specific documents or information are missing | The refusal letter should identify what is needed and how to provide it | Submit a complete, truthful response through the channel specified by the embassy or consulate |
| The case requires administrative processing | Additional case-specific review is required; the post will communicate when it is complete | Follow the post's instructions and do not treat public wait-time tables as a prediction for the case |
| The instruction is unclear | Global guidance cannot reconstruct a case-specific request | Use the official contact channel of the post that handled the application, without sending case data to VisaOS |

## A refusal can still be reconsidered

The Department explains that a fully executed visa application has been issued or refused. While administrative processing continues, the case remains refused because eligibility has not yet been established. After the requested information is received or administrative processing ends, the officer may conclude that the applicant qualifies, or may conclude that the applicant remains ineligible.

This distinction matters: calling every 221(g) case “approved pending processing” is inaccurate, but describing every 221(g) refusal as permanently finished is also inaccurate.

## Missing documents and administrative processing are different

### When information was requested

Provide exactly what the officer requested, using the stated submission method. The Department says an applicant has one year from the refusal date to submit requested additional information. If the required information is not provided within that period, the applicant must reapply and pay another application fee.

The one-year rule applies to responding to a request for additional information. It is not a promised deadline for the government to finish administrative processing.

### When no applicant document was requested

Administrative processing may depend on information from sources other than the applicant. Sending unsolicited documents or repeatedly contacting unrelated offices is not presented by the Department as a way to accelerate that review. If the situation presents a unique hardship, the Department says to inform the consular section where the application was made; it does not promise expedited completion.

## No reliable processing-time promise exists

The Department states that administrative-processing duration varies with the individual circumstances of each case. Its published nonimmigrant appointment and processing estimates do not include administrative-processing time or passport return time. For that reason, the VisaOS guide to [appointment wait-time figures](/us/visa-wait-times/) cannot be used to predict a 221(g) outcome date.

Avoid non-refundable travel commitments based on an assumed completion date. A case tracker, forum report or another applicant's timeline cannot establish when a different case will finish.

## 221(g) compared with 214(b)

| Question | 221(g) | 214(b) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Why was the visa refused? | The record was incomplete or additional administrative processing was required | Eligibility for the nonimmigrant category and/or the applicable temporary-intent requirement was not established |
| Can the same application be reconsidered? | Yes, after requested information or completion of administrative processing | No; the Department says the specific case is closed |
| Does the applicant file an appeal? | No appeal is described; follow the officer's request or processing instructions | The Department states there is no appeal process |
| Does either result guarantee later issuance? | No | No |

## Practical scenarios

### The letter requests one document

Use the exact post-designated submission method and retain a private copy and delivery record. Do not upload the refusal sheet, passport page or case number to a public issue for troubleshooting.

### The case status displays “Refused” during administrative processing

That status is consistent with the Department's explanation that the application remains refused until eligibility is established. It does not, by itself, reveal whether the final decision will be issuance or continued ineligibility.

### Travel has become urgent

Use the official contact method for the consular section that owns the case and explain the documented hardship. An urgent circumstance does not guarantee faster administrative processing or approval.
