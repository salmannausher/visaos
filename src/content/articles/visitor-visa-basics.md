A **B-1 visa is generally for temporary business activities**, a **B-2 visa is generally for tourism, visits and certain other temporary personal purposes**, and a **combined B-1/B-2 visa may be used for qualifying activities in either category**. None of these classifications authorizes ordinary U.S. employment, degree study or permanent residence. The classification must fit the genuine purpose of each trip, and a visa never guarantees admission at the border.

This guide explains the general process in the U.S. Department of State's [current visitor visa guidance](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/tourism-visit/visitor.html). Procedures can differ by U.S. embassy or consulate, so always follow the official instructions for the post handling the application.

## B-1, B-2 and B-1/B-2 compared

| Classification | General purpose | Examples in Department of State guidance | Important limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **B-1** | Temporary business activity | Consulting with business associates; attending a scientific, educational, professional or business convention or conference; settling an estate; negotiating a contract | It is not a general work visa. The activity must fit a permitted business-visitor purpose rather than ordinary U.S. employment. |
| **B-2** | Tourism and certain temporary personal visits | Tourism or vacation; visiting family or friends; medical treatment; participating in social events; participating as an unpaid amateur in certain musical, sports or similar events; taking a short recreational, non-credit course | Degree or credit-bearing study and employment require a different classification. Medical-treatment applicants may be asked for additional evidence. |
| **B-1/B-2** | A combined classification covering qualifying B-1 or B-2 travel | A traveler may use it for an eligible temporary business trip or an eligible tourism/visit purpose | The combined label does not make an otherwise prohibited activity permissible. The actual purpose of the trip still controls. |

These are examples, not a personalized eligibility determination. The Department of State's [B-1 fact sheet](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/business/b-1-fact-sheet.html) describes B-1 as appropriate for specified business activities that do not involve gainful employment in the United States. If an activity does not clearly fit, identify the appropriate visa category before applying rather than stretching a visitor classification.

## What visitor visas generally do not permit

The Department of State lists the following among purposes that are not permitted on visitor visas:

- degree or credit-bearing study;
- employment in the United States;
- paid performances, or professional performances before a paying audience;
- arrival as a crewmember on a ship or aircraft;
- work as foreign press, radio, film, print journalism or other information media;
- permanent residence in the United States; and
- birth tourism when the primary purpose of travel is giving birth in the United States to obtain U.S. citizenship for the child.

The limits require careful wording. A short recreational course that does not provide degree credit may qualify under B-2. The Department of State also describes narrow, fact-specific B-1 situations, including for some personal or domestic employees. Those exceptions have their own conditions; they do not create general permission to work. A person planning study, employment, media work, a performance or another specialized activity should use the official [U.S. visa category guidance](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas.html) and obtain qualified legal advice where the facts are uncertain.

## General visitor visa application roadmap

The exact order and method vary by embassy or consulate. This is a general roadmap, not a substitute for the instructions of the post where the application will be handled.

### 1. Identify the actual purpose of travel

Choose the classification based on what the traveler intends to do, not simply on which visa appears easiest to obtain. If a trip has more than one purpose, each planned activity still needs to be permissible under the requested classification.

### 2. Complete Form DS-160 online

Submit the [DS-160 Online Nonimmigrant Visa Application](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/forms/ds-160-online-nonimmigrant-visa-application.html) and keep the confirmation page with its barcode. The Department of State says applicants do not need to print the entire application. Information in the DS-160 and the personal interview is used by the consular officer to assess eligibility.

### 3. Provide a compliant photograph

The DS-160 process includes uploading a photograph that meets the Department of State's current requirements. If the confirmation page shows an **X** where the photo should appear, the visitor visa instructions say to bring one printed photograph in the required format. Follow the official photo specifications rather than relying on a commercial photographer's assumptions.

### 4. Pay the applicable fee and schedule the appointment

Pay the non-refundable visa application fee using the process specified by the embassy or consulate. Check the Department of State's [current fee schedule](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/fees/fees-visa-services.html) instead of relying on an old amount. Depending on nationality, an additional visa issuance fee may apply after approval.

Applicants must schedule their own interview and should use the official embassy or consulate instructions. Wait times vary by location, season and visa category. The Department of State says applicants should generally schedule at the U.S. embassy or consulate in the country where they live; applying elsewhere may make it more difficult to demonstrate eligibility.

### 5. Assemble the documents required for the interview

The general visitor visa page identifies these documents:

- a passport valid for travel to the United States and generally valid for at least six months beyond the intended stay, unless a country-specific agreement provides an exemption;
- the DS-160 confirmation page;
- the application-fee payment receipt, if the local process requires payment before the interview; and
- one printed photograph only when the DS-160 photo upload failed, following the official photo instructions.

Every applicant needs an individual application, including family members listed in another person's passport.

### 6. Be ready for additional evidence that may be requested

Required documents are not the same as every item a consular officer might request. Additional evidence may be requested to establish:

- the purpose of the trip;
- intent to leave the United States after the trip; and
- the ability to pay the costs of the trip.

The Department of State says evidence of employment or family ties may be sufficient to show the purpose of travel and intent to return. If the applicant cannot cover all costs, evidence that another person will pay some or all of them may be provided. The appropriate evidence depends on the applicant's circumstances and the post's instructions; there is no single universal supporting-document checklist.

### 7. Attend the interview, if required

Interviews are generally required, although limited exceptions may apply under current policy. A consular officer can require an interview from any applicant. Digital fingerprint scans are usually taken during the process, with practices varying by location.

During the interview, answer accurately and consistently. Do not purchase non-refundable tickets or make final travel plans merely because an application has been submitted; the Department of State advises waiting until the visa has been issued.

### 8. Receive the case outcome

The consular officer decides whether the applicant qualifies under U.S. law. A case may be approved, refused or require additional administrative processing. If approved, the applicant may need to pay an applicable issuance fee and arrange return of the passport and visa according to local instructions. Approval of the application does not itself authorize entry into the United States.

## Visa validity is not the permitted period of stay

A visa's **validity period** is the time during which its holder may travel to a U.S. port of entry and request admission. It does not determine how long the person may remain in the United States, and it does not guarantee entry.

At the port of entry, U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) decides whether to admit the traveler and, if admitted, the authorized period of stay. That admission record—often shown on the admission stamp or electronic Form I-94—controls the permitted stay. The Department of State's [visa validity guidance](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/visa-expiration-date.html) explains that a visa can expire while a person is in the United States without automatically ending an authorized stay, provided the CBP-authorized admission period has not expired and the person continues to comply with status.

## An invitation letter is not required

The Department of State expressly says that a letter of invitation or Affidavit of Support is **not needed** to apply for a visitor visa and is not one of the factors used to decide whether to issue or deny the visa. The applicant must qualify based on ties abroad and their own circumstances, not assurances from U.S. family members or friends.

This does not mean supporting evidence is never relevant. For example, an applicant who cannot pay the full cost of the trip may show evidence that another person will cover some or all expenses. That is different from treating an invitation letter as a required or decisive document.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a B-1/B-2 visa allow both business and tourism?

It can be used for qualifying temporary activities in either B-1 or B-2 categories. The planned activity for a particular trip must still be permitted. The combined classification does not authorize employment, degree study or another prohibited purpose.

### Can I work in the United States on a B-1, B-2 or B-1/B-2 visa?

Not in ordinary U.S. employment. B-1 covers specified temporary business activities that do not amount to gainful employment in the United States. Because official guidance contains narrow, fact-specific B-1 situations, do not rely on a generic label such as “business meeting” when the activity actually involves productive work or employment.

### Can I study while visiting?

Degree or credit-bearing study is not permitted on a visitor visa. Department of State guidance identifies a short recreational course that does not award degree credit as an example of a B-2 activity. If education is a primary purpose or provides academic credit, review the appropriate student visa category.

### Is a flight booking required before the visa interview?

The general visitor visa required-document list does not include a purchased flight ticket. The Department of State says not to make final travel plans or buy tickets until the visa has been issued.

### Is an invitation letter from a U.S. resident required?

No. The Department of State states that an invitation letter or Affidavit of Support is not required and is not a factor in the issuance or denial decision. An officer may still request evidence about the trip, the applicant's intent to depart and how costs will be paid.

### Does a valid visa guarantee entry or a specific length of stay?

No. A valid visa permits travel to a port of entry to request admission. CBP makes the admission decision and determines the authorized period of stay for each admitted trip.

### Is an interview always required?

Interviews are generally required, but limited exceptions may apply under current rules. The responsible embassy or consulate determines the local process, and a consular officer may require an interview from any applicant.

### What additional evidence may be requested for medical treatment?

The Department of State says a consular officer may request a medical diagnosis from a local physician, a letter from a U.S. physician or medical facility describing the proposed treatment and its projected time and cost, and proof that transportation, medical and living expenses will be paid. These are possible additional items for a medical-treatment case, not a universal checklist for every visitor visa applicant.
