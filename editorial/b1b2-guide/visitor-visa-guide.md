A **B-1 visitor visa is for qualifying temporary business activities**, a **B-2 visitor visa is for tourism and certain personal purposes**, and a **combined B-1/B-2 visa covers qualifying activities under either category**. None is a general U.S. work visa, a route to ordinary degree study, or permission to live in the country permanently. A visa allows travel to seek admission; it does not guarantee entry.

This independent guide explains the official requirements and includes practical preparation insights from three supplied educational videos featuring Travis Feuerbacher, whom his firm's biography and a Washington University event identify as a former consular officer and immigration attorney. His observations are attributed perspectives, **not U.S. government policy, legal advice, or approval guarantees**. For current rules, follow the [Department of State's visitor visa guidance](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/tourism-visit/visitor.html) and the instructions for the U.S. embassy or consulate processing your case.

## B-1 vs. B-2 vs. combined B-1/B-2

| Classification | Typical purpose | Examples | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **B-1** | Temporary qualifying business | Business consultations, contract negotiations, eligible professional conferences | Not ordinary U.S. employment or productive labor outside a recognized B-1 allowance |
| **B-2** | Tourism and temporary personal visits | Holidays, visiting family and friends, certain medical treatment, short recreational non-credit classes | Not regular academic study or employment |
| **B-1/B-2** | Eligible activities in either category | A conference trip with sightseeing, or separate eligible business and tourism trips | The combined label does not authorize otherwise prohibited activities |

Some nationalities may be eligible to travel under the Visa Waiver Program rather than seeking a B visa. Different special rules may apply to certain other travelers. See the [official visitor visa page](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/tourism-visit/visitor.html).

## Who qualifies? Three useful questions to prepare for

A former visa officer's practical framework is to ask whether the trip has a permitted **purpose**, whether its **financing** is credible, and whether the applicant can establish a **temporary intention to visit**. These are preparation questions, not an exhaustive legal test; other eligibility or inadmissibility grounds may apply.

### 1. Is the purpose permitted?

The officer needs to understand what you actually plan to do. A genuine holiday, an eligible family visit, a qualifying meeting, or an appropriate conference can fit the visitor classification. Ordinary employment, degree study, or an intention to relocate normally cannot. If your trip includes several activities, make sure **each** fits the proposed classification.

A broad itinerary is not necessarily a problem. But be prepared to explain the main cities, approximate length of stay and purpose without fabricating reservations or presenting an invented story.

### 2. How will the visit be paid for?

Applicants may be asked to show how airfare, lodging, food, transport, and any medical expenses will be covered. The State Department does **not** prescribe a universal bank-balance amount that guarantees approval. Evaluate the financial arrangements in relation to the actual trip, not an internet formula.

If someone else will pay for part or all of the trip, evidence of that support may be relevant. Sponsorship by a U.S. friend or family member does not, by itself, establish visa eligibility. See the [official document and funding guidance](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/tourism-visit/visitor.html).

### 3. Why is the visit temporary?

For B-visa applicants, INA 214(b) places the burden on the applicant to satisfy the applicable temporary-intent and classification requirements. Employment, a continuing business, close relationships, an established home, and other responsibilities may help explain the person's individual circumstances and plan to depart. **Property ownership is not mandatory**, and a bank statement, business license, or family relationship does not automatically prove eligibility.

The right preparation is to understand your own circumstances and explain them truthfully if relevant—not invent stronger ties. For the refusal rule and reapplication limits, see [Understanding a 214(b) Visa Refusal](/us/problems/214b/) and the [State Department's Visa Denials guidance](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/visa-denials.html).

## What can you do on a B-1/B-2 visitor visa?

| Activity | General treatment | Important nuance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sightseeing, holidays, visiting family or friends | Generally permitted under B-2 | The trip remains temporary |
| Medical treatment | Potentially permitted under B-2 | Medical plan and ability to meet costs may need evidence |
| Short recreational, non-credit classes | Generally permissible when incidental to a visit | Not a substitute for academic enrollment |
| Consulting business associates, negotiating contracts | Potentially permitted under B-1 | Cannot be ordinary unauthorized employment |
| Scientific, professional, educational or business conferences | Generally permitted under B-1 | Speaking, compensation and work duties may require further review |
| Certain independent research | May be permissible under B-1 | The actual activities and any compensation determine suitability |

### Conference participation and presentations

Attending a qualifying professional or educational conference can be permissible. Presenting research, delivering a lecture, receiving compensation, or performing services raises additional questions. An event invitation does not automatically authorize paid professional services. Check the [B-1 allowable uses fact sheet](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/business/b-1-fact-sheet.html) and obtain qualified advice when the proposed activity is not clearly covered.

### Business meetings versus actual work

B-1 permits specified business activities such as consultations and contract negotiations, **not ordinary employment**. Being paid by a foreign employer does not automatically make productive work physically performed in the United States permissible. If you will install equipment, deliver operational services, provide ongoing labor, work with U.S. clients, or perform paid speaking engagements, examine the specific applicable exception or appropriate work classification rather than relying on the label “business trip.”

### Recreational classes versus regular study

A short recreational cooking class during a holiday is an example of an activity that may be allowed on B-2. Enrolling in regular schooling or a degree- or credit-oriented academic program generally requires an appropriate student or other immigration classification. A course's short duration alone is not enough to make it a visitor activity.

## Activities that are generally not permitted

The [official visitor visa page](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/tourism-visit/visitor.html) identifies ordinary employment, formal academic study, certain paid performances, working as foreign media, entry as a crew member, and permanent residence among purposes outside ordinary visitor-visa use. Specialized exceptions exist and must be analyzed on their own terms.

### Myth: Paying property tax means children can attend school as visitors

**Incorrect.** Buying a home and paying property taxes do not change the immigration permissions attached to B-1/B-2 visitor status. Ordinary enrollment in a U.S. school is not made lawful by owning property. Families planning education should identify the appropriate student or dependent classification rather than using visitor status as a substitute.

### Pregnancy, medical treatment and birth tourism

The State Department's rule focuses on purpose: travel **primarily to give birth in the United States to obtain U.S. citizenship for a child** is not permissible on a visitor visa. The current visitor-visa page does not say pregnancy alone automatically bars a B visa. Legitimate medical-treatment travel is addressed separately and may require details of treatment and costs. Individual cases can be legally complex. See the [current State Department visitor-visa guidance](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/tourism-visit/visitor.html).

## How to apply: Step by step

### Step 1 — Confirm the right category and interview location

First establish that your intended activities fit a visitor visa. Under [July 15, 2026 State Department instructions](https://travel.state.gov/en/newsroom/visas/2026/adjudicating-nonimmigrant-visa--niv--applicants-in-their-country.html), nonimmigrant applicants should generally schedule an interview in their country of **nationality or residence**, or at a designated post where applicable. Applying elsewhere may create difficulties and longer waits. Exceptions are limited; follow the current embassy instructions rather than chasing an apparently shorter wait in another country.

### Step 2 — Prepare and submit the DS-160

Complete the [DS-160 online](https://ceac.state.gov/genniv/), answer all questions accurately, and review the application before submission. Keep the application ID secure, save your work regularly and retain the barcode confirmation page. You do not normally need to print the entire application. See the VisaOS [DS-160 overview](/us/ds-160/) and [saving and returning](/us/ds-160/save-and-return/) guides.

Once submitted, a DS-160 cannot be treated like an ordinary editable draft. The [official FAQ](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/forms/ds-160-online-nonimmigrant-visa-application/ds-160-faqs.html) describes limited cases of post-directed reopening; no global rule governs every applicant's appointment barcode updates. If you discover an error, see [Correcting a submitted DS-160](/us/ds-160/correct-after-submission/). If you lose your barcode page, see [Reprinting a DS-160 confirmation page](/us/ds-160/confirmation-page/).

### Step 3 — Pay the correct fees and schedule the interview

Use the scheduling provider linked from your responsible embassy or consulate's official website. Posts can use authorized external portals and different payment procedures. Avoid unofficial slot brokers and unsolicited payment links.

As checked on **November 4, 2026**, the State Department's standard non-petition-based B-visa application processing fee is **US$185** and non-refundable. Depending on the applicant and applicable rules, an issuance fee or other nationality-specific requirement may also apply. Check the [official fee schedule](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/fees/fees-visa-services.html), [reciprocity information](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/fees/fees-reciprocity-tables.html) and the consular post before paying. Some nationalities are currently subject to B-visa [bond requirements](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/News/visas-news/countries-subject-to-visa-bonds.html); a bond does not guarantee issuance, and applicants must not pay unless a consular officer directs them through the official process.

Appointments may be scarce. Published [visa wait times](/us/visa-wait-times/) are estimates, not reservations. Emergency requests or any temporary expedited-appointment program have separate eligibility and availability limits; see [Expedited visa appointments](/us/appointments/expedited-appointment/).

### Step 4 — Gather required documents and prepare

The State Department generally identifies a passport valid for travel and usually at least six months beyond the planned stay (unless an applicable exemption), the DS-160 confirmation page, an applicable fee receipt, and a printed photo if the upload failed or the post directs it. Each applicant requires an individual application. Follow the post's current instructions.

Additional information can be requested about purpose, departure intention and finances. Employment evidence, business records, family ties or other documents may help explain real circumstances, but **no single document list guarantees approval**. A letter of invitation or Affidavit of Support is **not required** and is not a deciding factor on its own. See the [official visitor-visa documentation section](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/tourism-visit/visitor.html).

### Step 5 — Attend the interview, when required

Most applicants require an interview, subject to narrow current waiver rules. The officer may review the DS-160, speak with the applicant and collect biometrics. Answer truthfully, directly and consistently with the actual circumstances; add relevant context when it helps answer the question, without delivering unrelated speeches or rehearsed invented scripts.

**Former officer's perspective:** Travis Feuerbacher encourages applicants to explain their real circumstances rather than assuming one-word answers are always best. This is a communication observation, not an official requirement or demonstrated way to improve approval odds.

### Step 6 — Understand the decision and passport return

A case can result in visa issuance, a refusal under a particular legal provision, or a 221(g) refusal involving additional documents or administrative processing. **221(g) is a refusal while the necessary information or processing is pending**, not a guaranteed approval. A **214(b) refusal** has no appeal process for that application; a new application may be possible under the applicable rules. See [214(b)](/us/problems/214b/) and [221(g)](/us/problems/221g/) explanations. Do not buy non-refundable tickets solely because you have applied or because an officer appears positive at interview.

### Step 7 — Travel, request admission and comply with your stay

Having a visa is not the same as being admitted. U.S. Customs and Border Protection decides whether to admit the traveler and for how long. Check your admission record using the [official CBP I-94 website](https://i94.cbp.dhs.gov/), and comply with the **Admit Until Date**, unless the period of stay is lawfully changed. Never assume a six-month admission; the period is determined for the individual entry. A visa valid for several years never authorizes continuous residence for all those years. Read the [State Department's visa-expiration explanation](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/visa-expiration-date.html).

## Interview do's and don'ts: practical preparation

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| Describe your actual temporary purpose | Invent a story or a fake itinerary |
| Know what you submitted in the DS-160 | Assume a scripted response is mandatory |
| Explain funding arrangements accurately | Assume a specific bank balance guarantees approval |
| Provide relevant context when asked | Hide material facts or provide unrelated speeches |
| Follow the post's document instructions | Assume an invitation letter decides the case |
| Accept that the officer evaluates eligibility individually | Expect any former-officer “formula” to guarantee issuance |

**Example topics, not sample answers to memorize:** Why this trip? What activities are planned? How will costs be met? How long do you intend to visit? What will you do after returning? Give truthful answers based on your own circumstances.

## Common questions

### Is there a required bank balance?

No globally prescribed bank balance guarantees a B visa. Relevant finances depend on the actual trip, the applicant's situation and any legitimate third-party support.

### Is a U.S. invitation letter mandatory?

No. The Department of State says it is not required and is not itself a factor deciding issuance. The applicant must establish personal eligibility.

### Can I attend a conference?

Often yes, under B-1, when the specific activities are eligible. Additional paid work, speaking duties or compensation may change the analysis.

### Can I work remotely for an overseas employer?

Do not assume foreign payment alone authorizes work performed while physically in the United States. The nature of the activity and the applicable rules matter; obtain qualified immigration advice for a proposed working arrangement.

### Can I take a recreational class?

A short non-credit recreational class incidental to tourism may be permissible. Regular academic or degree study generally requires an appropriate classification.

### Can I apply at another country's U.S. embassy?

Check the July 2026 location-of-application guidance and current post rules first. Nationality, residence, designated-post arrangements and limited exceptions matter. A shorter listed wait time is not enough to assume the choice is appropriate.

### Is a six-month stay automatic?

No. CBP determines your period of admission. Check your I-94 instead of relying on the visa's expiration date.

### What if I previously had a visa refusal?

Review the actual provision cited in the refusal notice. An earlier 214(b) refusal differs materially from 221(g); neither the passage of time nor a new document automatically ensures approval.
