# Editorial and Visa Guidance Policy

Priority official sources: U.S. Department of State (travel.state.gov), applicable U.S. embassy/consulate sites, and relevant official scheduling instructions. Government sources outrank forum reports for rules. Experience reports may help discover questions, not establish legal requirements.

For each material visa claim record: exact source URL, date checked, visa classification, applicant nationality/residence/post where relevant, applicable date/version, uncertainties and exceptions. Never infer a fixed required-document list from an anecdote. Maintain revision history and dependent-page references.

Workflow: keyword brief → official source research → claim-evidence map → draft → independent factual and editorial QA → founder approval → build checks → publish → maintenance queue. Changed web pages or broken URLs trigger review, not automatic rule changes.

No fake legal expertise, false government affiliation, approval probabilities or guarantees. Distinguish generally available information from individualized legal advice; refer unusual/high-stakes situations to government sources or qualified immigration professionals. Refusals, 221(g), and entry/eligibility are sensitive topics needing added care.

Do not copy government or competitor text wholesale; paraphrase accurately and add original organizational utility. Accessibility, clear language, meaningful examples, useful tables/decision trees and source transparency are more valuable than word count.

## Publishing states

- `draft`: research and writing are incomplete; no public route.
- `pending-review`: ready for independent accuracy/editorial review; no public route.
- `approved`: founder-approved content. A route is generated only when `noindex` is also `false` and all metadata/source gates pass.
- `rejected`: retained only when useful for editorial history; no public route.

Approval requires a named reviewer, review date, approval date and next-review date. Publishing code must not infer approval from the presence of a Markdown file.

## Freshness defaults

The content validator flags official sources for re-verification after 45 days for high-volatility sources, 180 days for medium-volatility sources and 365 days for low-volatility sources. An article-specific `reviewDueAt` date may require an earlier check. A stale source is a build error when referenced by publishable content and a warning when it affects only non-published drafts.
